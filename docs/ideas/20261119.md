## What the literature-span skill is for, and what it may draw on

Relayed to this seat `design-macbook-20261117-1` by the status-report session (`local_2f39e32b-c7ca-45ec-9e38-f8d7c66827c0`, no seat). This seat did not hear these words. [I had instructed that a skill be made that can verify cases like the ones just passed arbitrarily: the one that works out the error range by consulting suitable materials, such as papers on other topics that deal with similar systems, manuals and so on.]

What the words settle, on this seat's reading: the skill the person asked for on 2026-11-18 ("Tolerances from the span of comparable published values", `20261118.md`) is meant to check cases that were passed by a person's judgement, such as those named above. A pass given by hand stays on the record as the person's judgement on that build. A span the skill later derives does not change that record.

What the words do not settle, and why this seat did not act on the relay: the recorded branch takes "the same quantity under the same conditions" in comparable published studies (`20261118.md`, and the root `CLAUDE.md`). Papers on other topics that treat a similar system, and manuals, reach wider. Whether they enter the span, and on what terms, amends the standing rule. This seat puts that to the person directly, and records the answer below when it is given.

This seat asked the person directly: "Should the literature span also draw on papers about similar systems on other topics, and on manuals?" The person answered with the button this seat wrote:

> Yes, widen it

What the words settle: branch 3 of the standing rule for tolerances is widened. The span is taken from suitable materials:
- published studies of the same quantity on the same system;
- papers on other topics that treat a similar system;
- the maker's manuals.

Each value, or each error range a source states, is read through `research-materials` and written as a material card. Before values are set side by side, `classify-knowledge` writes how each source's system and quantity compare with the task's. The interval stays [lowest, highest] ("Min–max of values", `20261118.md`). The task records it as `chosen_by: rule` with `from: literature_span`. Where no suitable material gives a value or a range, branch 3 yields nothing and the next branch applies. The skill is meant to check, among others, the cases the person passed by judgement (CFD round four's three values, the GROMACS water count). A span it derives is a check beside those passes, and the passes stay on the record.

What the words do not settle:
- what makes a material "suitable" (적절한) for a given quantity, beyond its relation being written;
- whether each study's own uncertainty widens the interval;
- whether a span the skill derives overturns a pass the person gave by judgement. It does not on this seat's reading; that is the person's to say.

The person then said in this seat's session, of the GROMACS water count:

It is the person's example of how far "similar" stretches for one quantity. It sets no rule for other quantities.

## A source's stated uncertainty widens the span

The span skill of task `028` (`literature_span@v1`) took a value that a source gives with an uncertainty beside it as the value alone. Its seat asked whether such a value enters as a range (`meta_agent/inbox/seats/20261119-meta-macbook-20261119-1-2.json`). This seat asked the person: "Should a source's stated uncertainty widen the span interval?" The person answered with the button this seat wrote:

> Widen by uncertainty

What the words settle: in branch 3 of the standing rule for tolerances, a value that its source states with an uncertainty enters the span as the range from the value less that uncertainty to the value plus it. The interval is still [lowest, highest] over all the edges ("Min–max of values").

What this seat reads into the words, as its reading:
- The uncertainty is taken as the source states it, in the quantity's unit. No multiplier enters, and none is recalled.
- The entry's `compares` says what kind of uncertainty the source states (a standard deviation, an expanded uncertainty, a bound), as `classify-knowledge` writes.
- One value with a stated uncertainty is one stated range, so it can give a span by itself, as a stated error range already does.

What the words do not settle: which of a source's numbers is its value for the quantity, and whether a span of two values known no finer than their sources' time steps should judge.
